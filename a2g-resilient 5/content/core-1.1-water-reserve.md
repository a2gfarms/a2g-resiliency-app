### THE STEPS *(fixed — the authored core, identical in all three profiles)*

**1. Figure out your number.**
Start with one gallon, per person, per day. Half of that is for drinking; the other half covers cooking, brushing teeth, keeping clean. For two weeks, that's 14 gallons a person. Don't forget the others under your roof — and yes, pets count. Write your number down before you buy anything. Everything else here is just hitting that number in a way that fits your life.

**2. Get containers you can trust.**
Use containers actually made for water — food-grade, sturdy, sealable. This is the one place we'd ask you not to improvise. Old milk jugs, bleach bottles, anything that held something else: skip them. They break down, they leak, and they can leave you worse off than no water at all. The right containers cost a little and last for years. Buy once, store for good.

**3. Fill it, treat it, seal it.**
If your water comes from a treated municipal tap, you can fill straight from the faucet — it's already safe to store. Add a water preserver if you'd like to stretch the storage life, following the label exactly. Seal each container tight, and write the date on it with a marker. **⟦VERIFY⟧** *(treatment specifics — see note)* If your water comes from a well or an untreated source, you'll want an extra step before storing — your coach can walk you through it for your exact setup.

**4. Put it somewhere cool, dark, and off the ground.**
Heat and sunlight are what age stored water, so the back of a closet beats a hot garage in July. Keep it off bare concrete if you can — a board or a shelf does the trick — and away from anything with strong fumes like fuel, paint, or cleaning supplies, since some plastics can pick up odors over time. Cool, dark, and boring is exactly what you want here.

**5. Set the reminder that keeps it fresh.**
Stored water doesn't really "go bad" if it was clean and sealed, but it's smart to refresh it about once a year so it always tastes right and you know your system works. Put a recurring reminder on your phone right now — pick a date you'll remember, like a birthday or the first day of a season. This is the step most people skip, and it's the one that turns a one-time chore into something you can actually count on.

## PROFILE A — APARTMENT / RENTER
*(Limited space, landlord rules, often a tighter budget. The proof that resilience doesn't need land.)*

**Why this matters for you:**
You're working with limited space and a landlord's rules, so we're skipping barrels and bulky gear entirely. Your whole two-week reserve can tuck into a closet or slide under the bed — no plumbing, no fixtures, nothing your lease would ever flag. People assume you need a basement or a yard to be ready. You don't. You need a corner you control and about an hour. Let's use them.

**Time:** about an hour · **Target:** ~14 gallons (one person) · **Level:** beginner-friendly

**Constraint callout — Lease-safe by design:**
Nothing here is permanent or visible. No holes, no hookups, no marks on the walls. If you moved out tomorrow, it all comes with you. Fully renter- and HOA-safe.

**What you'll need (honest cheap path first):**
- Three 5-gallon stackable water jugs — food-grade, BPA-free — *about $21*
- A small bottle of water preserver drops — *about $4*
- Your tap — *free, you've already got it*
- **Starter total: around $25**

*Upgrade path (when you're ready):* a single 7-gallon container with a built-in spigot makes pouring easier, and a case of sealed bottled water is a fine no-effort starting point if jugs feel like too much at once.

**Coach starter questions (the seeded chips):**
- "My closet is tiny — where does this actually go?"
- "Can I really not reuse old jugs?"
- "Is my tap water safe to just store like that?"

## PROFILE B — SUBURB / HOUSE
*(Some storage, a garage or utility room, a bit more budget. Room to build a real household system.)*

**Why this matters for you:**
You've got a garage or a utility space and a little more room to work, so we'll build something sturdier than a closet kit — a real household reserve with rotation built in, so it stays fresh without you babysitting it. You don't need to turn the garage into a warehouse. A couple of smart containers and a simple rotation habit covers your whole household through most of what an outage or a boil-water notice will throw at you.

**Time:** about two hours · **Target:** ~56 gallons (household of four) · **Level:** easy with a helper

**Constraint callout — Works within your HOA:**
Everything here lives inside your garage or utility space — nothing visible from the street, nothing an HOA has any say over. If you flagged an HOA, your coach keeps every suggestion inside the lines.

**What you'll need:**
- Two 7-gallon rigid carriers, stackable and spigot-ready — *about $60*
- One 30-gallon barrel with a bung/cap — your main store — *about $55*
- A siphon pump for easy rotation, no heavy lifting — *about $15*
- Water preserver plus test strips — *about $18*
- **Starter total: around $148**

*Upgrade path:* a second barrel doubles you to a month of reserve, and a simple barrel rack keeps everything off the concrete and easy to rotate.

**Coach starter questions:**
- "How often do I actually need to rotate the barrel?"
- "Where in the garage is safest to keep this?"
- "Do I need to treat it if it's just city water?"

## PROFILE C — RURAL / ACREAGE
*(Space and often well water — which changes the safety picture. More capacity, more responsibility.)*

**Why this matters for you:**
You've got the space most people wish they had — but if your water comes from a well, an outage means your pump stops and the tap goes dry, so stored water matters *more* out here, not less. We'll build real capacity, and because well water isn't treated the way city water is, we'll be a little more careful about how you store and refresh it. You're not starting from zero — you're starting from "capable," and we'll build toward genuinely self-reliant.

**Time:** a half-day project · **Target:** 100+ gallons (household, with margin) · **Level:** straightforward, worth doing right

**Constraint callout — Well water needs an extra step:** **⟦VERIFY⟧**
Because your water isn't municipally treated, you'll want to treat it before long-term storage rather than filling straight from the tap. Your coach can walk you through the right method for your specific well — this is one we get exact, not approximate.

**What you'll need:**
- One or two 55-gallon food-grade barrels with bung wrench and siphon — *about $90–150*
- A larger-volume option worth considering: a 150–300 gallon water tank if you've got the space — *varies*
- Water preserver sized for large volume, plus test strips — *about $25*
- A way to treat well water for storage (your coach specifies the method) — **⟦VERIFY⟧**
- **Starter total: around $130 and up, scaling with capacity**

*Upgrade path:* this is where rainwater capture and a gravity-fed backup start to make real sense — but those are their own cores. Master stored reserve first.

**Coach starter questions:**
- "My water's from a well — what do I do differently?"
- "What happens to my water when the power's out?"
- "How much capacity is actually enough for my place?"
